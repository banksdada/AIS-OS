How to fill your diary without living on your phone

Most beauty businesses do not need more platforms. They need the ones they already use to work harder. Here is where I would start if I were running a lash, nail, brow, aesthetics or African hair business in Wigan or Manchester this week.

1. Finish your Google Business Profile

When someone searches for a beautician near them, Google Maps is often the first page they see. Add your real opening hours, the services you offer, a current photo set and one clear booking link. A half-filled profile makes a good business look closed. Check the profile from a phone, not just a laptop, because that is where most clients will find you.

2. Use one booking link everywhere

Put the same booking link in your Instagram bio, your Google profile, your email signature and your saved replies. If people have to hunt for how to book, many will simply message you instead. That is the enquiry you then have to answer by hand, often while your hands are busy.

3. Save answers to the five questions you always get

Prices, availability, where you are, whether a deposit is needed and what happens after the appointment. Write each answer once in plain language and reuse it. A quick, consistent reply feels professional, and it protects your time. Our Instagram auto-replies do this for you when you cannot get to your phone.

4. Show real work with a plain caption

Photograph the result in good light, then name the service and the area in the caption. "Braids, Wigan" or "Builder gel, Manchester" tells both people and search engines what you do and where. You do not need a photoshoot. You need honest, recent work that looks like the appointment someone will book.

5. Ask for a review at the right moment

The best time is when the client is looking at their result and is genuinely pleased. Ask then, and send the link to your Google profile. Reviews on Maps do more for local discovery than a post that disappears in an hour. Thank every reviewer, including the short ones.

6. Keep a rhythm you can actually maintain

Three useful posts a week beat fifteen posts in one burst and then silence. One result, one behind-the-scenes moment and one answer to a common question is enough to start. Batch the captions on a quiet afternoon and schedule them.

7. Automate the admin, not the service

The hands-on part of your work should stay human. Reminders, price questions, booking confirmations and follow-up messages are the parts to hand over. That is the whole idea behind Beauty Booked Up: less time on admin, more time with clients.
