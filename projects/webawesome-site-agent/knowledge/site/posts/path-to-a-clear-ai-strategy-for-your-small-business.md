# Path to a Clear AI Strategy for Your Small Business

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/path-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-small-business/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:21:14

Every small business owner we talk to in London has the same question right now: “Where do we actually start with AI?” The headlines make it sound like you’re already behind. The tool vendors make it sound urgent. But most SMEs don’t need a strategy deck — they need a clear, practical plan that saves time or money in the next 90 days. Here’s a five-step approach that works whether you’re a five-person team or fifty. No buzzwords. No six-month roadmaps. Just a sensible path from “we should probably look at AI” to “we’re already seeing results.” Step 1: List the tasks eating your team’s time Before you pick an AI tool, write down the everyday jobs that quietly drain hours. Common culprits in small businesses: Replying to the same customer questions over and over Drafting proposals, quotes, and follow-up emails Summarising meeting notes or long documents Transcribing customer calls Updating spreadsheets, CRMs, and project boards by hand Be honest about where the hours actually go. The right first use case is almost always one of these — repetitive, time-consuming, low-risk tasks that no one enjoys. Step 2: Pick ONE workflow to start with This is where most AI strategies fall apart. People try to do everything at once, pick three tools, build nothing properly, and call it a strategy. Pick one workflow. Then ask: Does it happen every week? Is the output quality easy to check? Can it run with a human reviewing the final result? If the answer is yes to all three, you’ve found your first project. Great early candidates are customer email replies, internal documentation, or content first drafts. Step 3: Define what “good” looks like — before you touch any tools Write down what success means. Things like: “Cut response time to customer enquiries from 4 hours to under 30 minutes” “Free up 8 hours a week for our operations manager” “Stop missing leads that come in after 6pm” This is the most skipped step in any AI rollout, and the reason most pilots fizzle out. If you can’t write down what you’re trying to achieve, you won’t know if it worked — and your team won’t either. Step 4: Match the tool to the workflow (not the other way around) Resist the urge to start with the product page of the latest shiny tool. Start with the problem and work backwards. For most SMEs, the practical first stack looks like: Off-the-shelf assistants for writing, summarising, and research A simple chatbot or helpdesk automation for routine customer questions Custom workflow automation tied into tools you already pay for If a task is core to how you make money — the way you handle enquiries, serve clients, or deliver a service — that’s where a tailored build or careful integration earns its keep. If it’s a back-office task, an off-the-shelf tool is usually fine. This is also where your website and app come in. AI features built directly into your customer-facing product (a booking assistant, a smart enquiry form, an in-app recommendation) tend to deliver more value than a separate internal tool. If you’re about to redesign your site or build something new, it’s worth designing the AI into the brief from day one — adding it later is more expensive. Our guide on the tasks for setting up a functional website covers what to think through before that build kicks off. Step 5: Measure, document, and decide what’s next Run the first workflow for 30 to 60 days. Then write up: What you tried What worked, what didn’t, and why The real time or money impact What you’d do differently next time Share it internally. This isn’t about proving the technology works — it’s about building the team’s confidence to try the next thing. A small business with two or three proven AI wins under its belt makes much smarter decisions than one still reading articles about it. After that, repeat the process with workflow number two. Each cycle gets faster because your team now knows what good looks like, which tools you trust, and where the real risks sit. What an AI strategy actually is for an SME It’s not a document. It’s not a software licence. It’s a habit of picking the right repetitive job, solving it well with the right tool, and moving on.
