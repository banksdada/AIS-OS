# The Business Website Redesign Checklist: 10 Steps Before You Brief a Designer

Why a redesign needs a plan, not just a mood board Most business owners know they need a website redesign but jump straight to picking colours and layouts. From our experience building sites for schools, healthcare providers, consulting firms, and dozens of other sectors, the projects that succeed share one thing in common: the requirements are nailed down clearly before a single line of code is written. When requirements stay vague, scope creeps, deadlines slip, and the final site never quite matches the original vision. A checklist forces those conversations to happen early. Below is the one we use with our own clients, broken into practical steps you can work through yourself. The 10-step business website redesign checklist 1. Define what type of site you actually need. Are you after a brochure site to build credibility, a lead generation machine, or an e-commerce store? Deciding this first shapes every other decision. Our guide on the three main types of websites walks through the differences in plain language. 2. Write down your top three business goals. “More sales” is too vague. “Generate a steady stream of qualified enquiries from organic search” is something you can actually design for. Every page, call-to-action, and form should be traceable back to one of these goals. 3. List the pages you genuinely need. It is tempting to copy a competitor’s sitemap, but most sites are bloated with pages no one visits. Be ruthless. Standard pages (home, about, services, contact, blog) are fine, but only add extras if they serve a goal. 4. Lock down your brand basics. Logo, colours, typography, tone of voice. If these aren’t already documented, get them sorted before briefing a designer. Otherwise you’ll burn hours debating shades of blue. 5. Audit the content you already have. Review your current pages, blogs, images, and case studies. Know what is good enough to keep, what needs rewriting, and what is missing entirely. Content is usually the biggest hidden cost in any redesign. 6. Map the user journey. Walk through the steps a visitor takes from landing on your homepage to becoming a customer. Where do they get stuck? Where do you lose them? Good websites convert because they are functional , not just because they look nice. 7. Specify functionality requirements early. Do you need booking forms, a quote calculator, payment processing, a member login, or CRM integrations? These features must be documented up front. Adding them mid-build is where budgets spiral. 8. Plan for SEO from day one. Too many redesigns wipe out years of search rankings because URLs change, redirects aren’t set up, and content gets reorganised without keyword research. Build your SEO plan alongside the design plan, not after. If budget is tight, our guide to affordable UK web design covers how to prioritise spend. 9. Set a realistic budget and timeline. A quality business website typically takes several weeks from kickoff to launch, depending on size and complexity. Be honest with your designer about both budget and deadline. Surprised stakeholders lead to compromised results. 10. Define how you’ll measure success. What does “done” look like? More enquiries, higher conversion rates, faster load times? Set baselines for your current site now so you can prove the redesign worked later. Common mistakes that derail business website redesigns After many redesign projects, we see the same pitfalls repeating. Treating the project as purely a visual refresh, ignoring mobile users, forgetting to set up 301 redirects, and launching without analytics tracking. The single biggest one? Skipping the requirements stage. Without clear, written-down requirements, every stakeholder assumes something different, and agreed expectations become impossible to manage.
