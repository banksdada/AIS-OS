# 5 Steps to a Clear AI Strategy for Your CIC

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/5-steps-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-cic/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:38:35

CICs are being told to “adopt AI” from every direction right now. Funders mention it in applications. Peer organisations talk about it at network meetings. Tool vendors pitch it relentlessly. But most small community organisations don’t have the spare time, budget, or technical team to figure out where AI actually fits into a mission already stretched thin. This is a practical 5-step approach to building an AI strategy that does the work your CIC actually needs — not the work someone is selling. Step 1: Map what your team actually does day-to-day Before you think about AI, document the processes that eat your team’s time. Grant reporting, beneficiary communications, scheduling, data entry, social media, meeting minutes, onboarding volunteers, chasing invoices. Most CIC teams we speak to are running on stretched capacity, and this exercise usually reveals three to five tasks where the bulk of working hours go. Start here because AI works best when it automates a process you already understand. You can’t automate chaos, and you shouldn’t try. Step 2: Pick one problem worth solving Don’t pick ten things. Pick one. The best AI projects for small organisations tend to share four qualities: They have clear inputs and outputs. They happen weekly or daily. They currently take more than two hours per week. They don’t require emotional judgement or sensitive human contact for the core decision. Examples worth piloting: drafting funding applications from past templates, summarising trustee meeting notes, generating social posts from a written brief, transcribing interviews with beneficiaries, building a first draft of an impact report. Step 3: Set up the guardrails before you pilot anything CICs hold sensitive data about beneficiaries, staff, and partners. You owe it to all of them to get this right before any AI tool touches real information. At a minimum, work through: A Data Protection Impact Assessment for any AI tool processing personal data. A clear written policy on what data can and cannot be pasted into public AI tools. A named person in the organisation who owns AI decisions. A simple, readable policy your team will actually follow. Skip this and a breach becomes existential. Funders walk. Beneficiaries lose trust. Reputation is harder to recover for a small CIC than for a private firm with a PR team. Step 4: Run a low-cost pilot and time-box it Give yourself six to eight weeks to test one tool on one task. Measure hours saved, quality of output, and any new problems created along the way. The goal of a pilot is not to prove AI is magic. It’s to find out, cheaply, whether this specific use case works for your organisation. The most common mistakes we see: Piloting too many things at once and drawing no clear conclusions. Not writing down the baseline — measure how long the task takes without AI first. Using free tools with no commercial agreement, then losing access mid-project when funding changes. Step 5: Decide what scales and what dies After the pilot, you’ll know. Some AI tools you’ll keep and expand. Others you’ll quietly retire. Write down what worked, what didn’t, and why. That document becomes the foundation of your real AI strategy, not a vague aspiration sitting in a drawer. The honest truth: most CICs need two or three practical AI tools, not a thirty-page strategy document. A working strategy fits on a single page and lists what you use, what each tool is for, who owns it, and what to do if it breaks.
