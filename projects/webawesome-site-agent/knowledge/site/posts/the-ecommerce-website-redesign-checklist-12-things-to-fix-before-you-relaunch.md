# The ecommerce Website Redesign Checklist: 12 Things to Fix Before You Relaunch

If your store is online, your website is your shop floor, cashier, and customer service desk rolled into one. When any of those break, you don’t just lose polish — you lose sales. A redesign is the best window you’ll get to fix the friction quietly draining your conversion rate. But ecommerce rebuilds are unforgiving. A one-second delay in page load can cost measurable revenue. A checkout that asks for too much pushes customers straight to a competitor. And unlike a brochure site, an ecommerce build has hundreds of moving parts — products, variants, stock, payments, shipping, taxes, reviews. Miss one, and the experience stumbles. Why This Checklist Matters Most ecommerce rebuilds don’t fail at the design stage. They fail at the friction stage: a checkout without Apple Pay, a PDP with a broken size guide, a redirect map that wipes out three years of SEO. The list below is the order we work through at WebAwesome before any store goes live. The ecommerce Website Redesign Checklist 1. Audit your current conversion funnel first. Pull 90 days of analytics before touching a pixel. Where do visitors drop off — the product page, the cart, or the checkout? Redesigning without this map is like repainting a house with a leaking roof. Our piece on functional websites that convert is worth a read. 2. Hit Core Web Vitals, especially on mobile. Aim for Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds, Cumulative Layout Shift below 0.1, and Interaction to Next Paint below 200ms. The majority of ecommerce traffic now comes from mobile, so test on a throttled 4G connection, not your office Wi-Fi. 3. Build product pages that earn their keep. Every PDP needs at least four images, a clear price, live stock status, shipping info, real customer reviews, and a sizing guide for anything worn. Sizing remains the single highest-impact addition for fashion ecommerce sites. 4. Plan site search, don’t bolt it on. Internal search users convert at meaningfully higher rates than casual browsers. Your search must handle synonyms (“sofa” vs “couch”), typos, and partial matches. Faceted filters become essential once a catalogue crosses a few hundred SKUs. 5. Offer guest checkout by default. Forcing account creation is one of the most reliable ways to lose a sale. Make guest checkout the default and let buyers save their details in one click after the order is placed. 6. Stack the payment methods your customers actually use. Cards are table stakes. Add Apple Pay and Google Pay to compress mobile checkout. For higher-ticket items, add Klarna, Clearpay, or your regional equivalent. 7. Show trust signals before you ask for the card. SSL is the bare minimum. Real trust comes from a visible returns policy, a phone number in the header, third-party review badges, and ideally a live activity widget on product and checkout pages. 8. Tighten your navigation. Mega menus look impressive and usually hide the products people actually want. Cap top-level categories at seven, and make sure every category is reachable in two clicks from the homepage. 9. Protect your SEO through the cutover. A redesign can quietly wipe out years of organic traffic if you don’t manage the migration. Set up 301 redirects for every URL that changes, map your title tags and meta descriptions, and submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console on day one. Our guide on improving website SEO covers the longer-term strategy. 10. Plan templates for messy real data. Real product feeds are messy. Your templates must handle missing images, out-of-stock items, and zero-review products without breaking layout or flow. 11. Test the checkout end-to-end before launch. Walk through on three devices with real cards, real addresses, and a real failure case — declined card, expired postcode, discount code that doesn’t apply. Too many redesigns go live with broken PayPal or shipping calculators. 12. Decide how you’ll measure success. Pick three to five KPIs before launch — conversion rate, average order value, add-to-cart rate, PDP bounce rate, Core Web Vitals — and review them weekly for the first month. A redesign without measurement is just a guess. What’s Common Across the Twelve Respect the buyer’s time, answer their doubts quickly, and get out of their way at the moment of payment. Miss that, and no amount of beautiful branding will save your conversion rate. This list is a starting point, but it doesn’t go deep on platform choice, third-party integrations, or the migration strategy that protects your existing traffic.
