# Steps to a Clear AI Strategy for Your Law Firm

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/steps-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-law-firm/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:22:34

AI is already showing up in your law firm, whether you’ve planned for it or not. Someone on your team is using ChatGPT to draft client emails. Another is experimenting with a document-review tool. The Solicitors Regulation Authority has published guidance on using AI safely, and clients are starting to ask how you’re using it. The firms that will get ahead aren’t the ones buying the shiniest tool. They’re the ones with a clear, defensible strategy. Here’s how to build one in five practical steps. Step 1: Audit What’s Already Happening Before you write a strategy, find out what’s already going on. Survey every fee earner, paralegal, and business services team member with one question: “How are you using AI today, even casually?” You’ll almost certainly find: Associates drafting first-pass arguments Marketing teams generating blog content IT trying out chatbots for client intake People copy-pasting client documents into free tools This audit isn’t about catching people out. It’s about understanding your real starting point. You can’t build a strategy on top of shadow IT you don’t know about. Step 2: Identify Use Cases That Map to Real Value Don’t start with tools. Start with the friction in your practice. For most UK firms, the highest-impact AI use cases sit in three buckets: Document-heavy work: contract review, due diligence, discovery Knowledge work: legal research, precedent libraries, matter summaries Client operations: intake triage, FAQ responses, scheduling Pick three candidate use cases. Score each on two things: hours saved per month, and risk if it goes wrong. Start with the ones that save real time AND carry manageable risk. Step 3: Build Compliance and Governance Into the Plan Early This is where most legal firm AI strategies fall apart. The SRA’s expectations are clear: you remain responsible for the advice your firm gives, regardless of which tool helped produce it. Your governance framework needs to cover: Client confidentiality: which data can and can’t enter third-party AI tools Data residency: UK/EU hosting where client data is involved Human-in-the-loop approval for anything client-facing A logged, auditable trail of AI-generated work product You don’t need a 40-page policy. You need a one-page rule the whole firm can follow, plus a named person who owns AI risk. Step 4: Pilot One Workflow Before You Scale Resist the urge to roll out firm-wide. Pick one workflow, one team, one quarter. Good pilot candidates: First-pass review of NDAs Summarising bundles for counsel Drafting client updates from case notes Define success upfront: hours saved, error rate, user satisfaction. Document what breaks. Talk to the people using it weekly. The pilot isn’t really a test of the tool, it’s a test of your firm’s ability to adopt new ways of working. Step 5: Measure Outcomes and Tie It to Client Value AI in a law firm is not a tech project. It’s a service-delivery project. Track three things beyond raw time savings: Faster turnaround on client deliverables Defects, complaints, or rework rates Whether clients notice, and whether it changes their perception of value If your AI rollout only makes internal processes slightly faster but doesn’t change what clients experience, you’ve added cost without adding value. The firms winning at this are using AI to offer faster initial drafts, fixed-fee scoping, or always-on client portals – things clients will actually pay more for.
