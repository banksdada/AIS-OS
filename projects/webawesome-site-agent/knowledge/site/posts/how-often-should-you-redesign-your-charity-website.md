# How Often Should You Redesign Your Charity Website?

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/how-often-should-you-redesign-your-charity-website/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:03:01

If your charity’s website still looks like it did in 2018, donors probably notice before you do. For most organisations, a website redesign is the kind of project that gets pushed from one year to the next — until traffic drops, donations slow, or a younger supporter quietly tells you the donate button doesn’t work on their phone. So how often should you actually redesign your charity website? The honest answer is that it depends on what you mean by “redesign.” A full visual rebuild is different from a periodic UX refresh, and both are different from small monthly improvements. Here’s a practical framework that works for small-to-mid-sized UK charities. The short answer: every 2 to 3 years for a full redesign For most charities, a full visual and structural redesign every 2 to 3 years is a sensible rhythm. That’s enough time to recover your investment, ride out a major campaign cycle, and let actual data — not opinions — tell you what isn’t working. If you’re a smaller charity with a static site under 20 pages, you can comfortably stretch that to 3 to 4 years . If you’re a national charity with high donation volume, active campaigns, and a young supporter base, plan closer to 2 years between major revamps. Don’t confuse “full redesign” with “improvement” The biggest mistake charities make is treating redesign as a one-off event. By the time you go three years without touching the site, you’ve usually accumulated a backlog of: Broken donation forms on mobile Outdated impact stories and trustee bios Stock photos that no longer reflect your community Accessibility issues that quietly exclude disabled donors Slow page loads that hurt your visibility on Google Most of these can be fixed without a full rebuild. Learning to treat your site as a working tool, not a brochure , is what separates charities that grow their online giving from those that don’t. The signs that say “redesign now, not later” Skip the calendar if any of these are true: Your donate page conversion rate has dropped for two consecutive quarters. Your mobile bounce rate is much higher than desktop — your layout is the problem, not your audience. Your CMS feels painful — your small team avoids updating it. You’re embarrassed to share it with major donors or trustees. It doesn’t pass basic accessibility checks — keyboard navigation, screen reader labels, colour contrast. If two or more apply, the 2–3 year rule goes out the window. You need a redesign this year. What to actually prioritise in a charity redesign Charity websites have a specific job: turn curious visitors into donors, volunteers, or campaign supporters. Every redesign decision should serve that. Focus your budget on: The donation flow. Fewer fields, clearer amounts, recurring giving defaults. This single page usually delivers the highest return of any change you can make. Mobile-first layouts. The majority of web traffic now comes from phones, and for under-35 supporters that share is even higher. Clear storytelling hierarchy. One cause, one ask, one next step per page. Trust signals — your registration number, recent impact data, real photos, named trustees. Speed. Slow sites lose donors. Aim for the page to be usable in under three seconds on a mid-range phone. The principles in our guide to user-friendly website layouts apply equally to commercial and nonprofit sites — clarity beats cleverness every time. The smaller refreshes you should do every year Between full redesigns, build a light maintenance habit: Quarterly: audit your top 10 pages for broken links, slow loads, and outdated content. Twice a year: refresh hero imagery, impact stats, and your most-read stories. Annually: review your donation page, your volunteer sign-up, and your homepage above the fold. None of this needs a big budget. Most of it needs an hour and a checklist. Charities that do this consistently rarely need an “emergency” redesign at all. A practical timeline for your next redesign If your last redesign was 2+ years ago, here’s a realistic plan: Month 1: audit your current site — traffic, conversions, top complaints from staff and donors. Months 2–3: define what success looks like. More donations? More volunteers? Better event sign-ups? Months 4–6: design and build with a partner who understands the third sector. Month 7: launch, measure, and commit to the quarterly refresh habit above. That gives you a working, redesigned site in under seven months — without the all-too-common two-year planning cycle that leaves sites stale before they ever go live.
