# 7 Signs Your Business Website Needs a Redesign (And What to Do Next)

Your website is often the first impression a prospect has of your business. In the UK, the majority of buyers, whether consumers or B2B decision-makers, will check you out online before they ever speak to you. If that experience is rough, slow, or plainly dated, you’ve lost them before you had a chance. The trouble is, most business owners don’t notice. They check the site on their own laptop, on a fast connection, once a year. By the time a redesign feels urgent, the damage has usually been building for months or even years. Here are seven signs it’s time to stop patching and start planning a proper redesign. They’re ordered from the most visible to the quietly costly. 1. Your Site Looks Older Than Your Competitors’ Open your site in one tab and three of your top competitors in the others. If yours sticks out like an old printed brochure in a current feed, that’s your first warning. Design conventions shift in small ways each year. After three or four years, the gap becomes obvious to anyone who isn’t looking at your site every single day. 2. It Doesn’t Work Properly on Mobile More than half of UK web traffic now comes from phones. If your site requires pinch-zooming, has overlapping text, or shows a desktop layout awkwardly squeezed into a 5-inch screen, you’re handing enquiries to competitors on a plate. “Mobile-friendly” in 2026 means genuinely thought-through on a phone, not a site that just doesn’t break. 3. Page Speed Is Sluggish If your homepage takes more than a few seconds to render on a typical 4G connection, visitors won’t wait. Slow sites cost you both rankings (Google has measured page experience signals, including Core Web Vitals, for years now) and conversions. Slowness is often a hosting, image, or build issue rather than a content one, and it’s usually fixable as part of a redesign. 4. You’re Not Getting Enquiries, or Not Enough of Them Your contact form is still there. Your phone number is still there. But the inbox is quiet and the phone isn’t ringing from the website. A redesign isn’t a magic fix for a quiet pipeline, but it’s often the moment you stop blaming “the market” and start rebuilding the site around what you actually need it to do: book calls, capture leads, sell. 5. It’s Painful to Update If updating a page means calling a developer, paying a monthly retainer, or risking breaking the layout, you stop doing it. The site goes stale. The blog dies. Events from last year are still labelled “upcoming.” A modern build should let a non-technical person publish a page, swap a photo, or fix a typo in under ten minutes. If yours doesn’t, the everyday cost is real. 6. It’s Not Built Around What You Actually Sell Businesses pivot. The site you launched three or four years ago sold three services. Now you sell eight, and three of them didn’t exist back then. If your navigation, home page, and service pages still reflect the old version of the business, prospects can’t quickly find what they came for. A redesign is the right moment to rethink the structure, not just the paint. 7. You’re Embarrassed to Send People to It This one’s brutally simple. Do you put your URL at the bottom of emails, or do you find excuses not to? If sales conversations involve you saying “the website’s a bit behind, but honestly we’re much better than this looks”, your website is actively working against you. Every apologetic moment is a deal cooling down without you noticing. What to Do Next If one or two of these rings true, you’re probably fine with a refresh: copy updates, new images, a sharper message on the existing pages. If three or more apply, a proper redesign is usually the more honest answer, and the one that pays for itself faster.
