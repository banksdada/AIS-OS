# 5 Steps to a Clear AI Strategy for Your Church

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/5-steps-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-church/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:38:22

Churches and faith communities across the UK are starting to ask the same question: how do we use AI sensibly without losing the personal, pastoral heart of what we do? It’s a fair question. The technology is moving quickly, and it’s easy to feel like you’re falling behind. The good news is you don’t need a Silicon Valley tech team to build a workable AI strategy. You need clarity on what you’re trying to solve, what your congregation actually needs, and a willingness to start small. Here are five steps to a clear AI strategy for your church. Step 1: Start With the Problem, Not the Tool The biggest mistake church leaders make with AI is starting with the technology. “We’ve heard chatbots are useful” or “ChatGPT can write sermons.” These statements lead nowhere good. Instead, list the friction points your team deals with week to week: New visitors sign up for your newsletter but never hear back Volunteers can’t find the rota quickly enough Sermon notes take staff hours every week Your website doesn’t tell people what to expect on a Sunday Pastoral care requests sit unanswered for days Pick the top two or three. Those are your AI candidates. Anything else is a distraction at this stage. Step 2: Audit Your Existing Digital Setup Before you add new tech, take stock of what you already have. Most churches we work with already run on a patchwork of Mailchimp, Google Drive, ChurchSuite, Planning Center, WhatsApp groups, and a website that hasn’t been updated in years. This matters because: New AI tools will need to plug into existing systems Bad input creates bad AI output Your team is already overwhelmed, so don’t add complexity without subtracting it If your website or admin portal is genuinely outdated, fix that first. A clear functional website setup is often the foundation everything else depends on. Step 3: Pick Tools That Fit Your Real Workflow Once you know your top problems and your current systems, choose tools that work with both. A few categories worth considering: Communications : AI-assisted email drafting, smart segmentation for newsletters, automated welcome sequences for first-time visitors Content : Sermon transcription, summary generation, blog and social post drafts from existing talks Pastoral care : Triage tools that help your pastoral team prioritise inbound messages Planning : Smart rota suggestions, conflict detection across volunteer schedules Resist the urge to buy ten subscriptions. One or two well-chosen tools that most of your team actually use will beat five tools nobody opens. Step 4: Run a Small Pilot Strategy is cheap. Pilots are honest. Pick one of your top problems, choose the simplest tool that addresses it, and test it for roughly 60 to 90 days with a small group of staff or volunteers. Sensible pilots for churches include: Using AI to draft weekly sermon summaries and running them past the preacher before publishing Trialling a chatbot on your website to answer the most common visitor questions Generating draft social posts from upcoming events and having a comms volunteer edit before posting Keep the pilot small. Keep the human in the loop. If it works, expand. If it doesn’t, you’ve learned cheaply. Step 5: Measure, Review, and Decide What Scales After the pilot, ask three questions: Did it actually save time, or did it just move the workload? Did the output match the tone and theology of your church? Are regular people in your congregation using it, or only the early adopters? Most churches will find that one or two AI use cases genuinely help. The rest can wait. A clear AI strategy isn’t about adopting every new tool. It’s about knowing which ones deserve a permanent place in your stack.
