# How Often Should You Redesign Your Coaching Website? (2026 Guide)

Updated: 2026-10-25T23:02:08

If your coaching website is more than a couple of years old, you’ve probably wondered whether it’s time for a refresh — or a full rebuild. The honest answer depends on a few factors, including your conversion rate, your niche, and how your audience now expects to interact with you. Here’s a practical guide to timing it right. The short answer A coaching website usually needs a meaningful redesign every 2–3 years. In between, you should be making smaller updates — new testimonials, refreshed copy, seasonal offers, fresh photos — every few months. Most agencies and platform providers recommend a similar 2–3 year cycle to keep pace with design expectations, browser changes, and how people actually browse. Warning signs it’s time for a redesign Don’t wait for the calendar to tell you. If any of these apply, your site is overdue: Conversion rates have dropped or plateaued. Getting traffic but few enquiries usually means the layout, offer, or booking flow is the problem. Coaches often leave a leaking site for over a year without noticing. It doesn’t work properly on mobile. Most coaching prospects research on a phone first. Pinch-zooming, slow loads, or broken menus will cost you bookings. Page speed is poor. Visitors abandon slow sites within seconds. If your pages take too long to render, you need a technical overhaul — not just a new logo. Your brand has changed. New niche, new audience, new methods, new qualifications. If the site doesn’t reflect who you are now, it’s actively working against you. You’re embarrassed to share the link. If you find yourself sending prospects to your LinkedIn profile instead of your own site, that’s the clearest signal of all. What you can refresh without a full redesign The highest-impact changes are usually quick wins: Update hero images and professional photos every 6–12 months Add fresh testimonials and case studies monthly Rotate your lead magnet each quarter Rework your call-to-action copy — “Book a call” versus “Book a free discovery session” can shift conversion noticeably Tighten your homepage so the value proposition is clear above the fold For more on layout choices that actually move the needle, see our piece on user-friendly website layouts . The coach-specific checklist Coaching sites sell trust, transformation, and time — usually in that order. Before you commit to a redesign, make sure the new site covers: A clear “who this is for” statement. Generic coaching pages underperform. Spell out the specific audience and outcome in the first scroll. Visible, frictionless booking. Whether you use Calendly, Acuity, or a custom flow, the path from landing to scheduled should be as short as possible. Proof that isn’t just quotes. Video testimonials, client outcomes, and before/after metrics beat generic text testimonials most of the time. A real lead magnet. A PDF guide, a short workshop replay, or a quiz — something prospects would actually exchange an email for. An About page that sells. Credentials alone don’t convert. Your bio needs personality, story, and the specific transformation you deliver. Conversion should drive every design decision. As we put it in our post on functional websites , a beautiful site that doesn’t book calls is just an expensive brochure. Common mistakes coaches make with redesigns Rebuilding for looks, not function. Pretty sites with weak CTAs and no clear booking path are the single most common coaching website mistake. Hiding pricing or process info. Most prospects want to know roughly what they’re buying before they book. Hiding everything behind a contact form loses warm leads. Choosing a template and calling it a day. Templates get you started, but the messaging, layout, and conversion flow still need to be deliberate. Skipping SEO. A redesign is the perfect time to fix titles, headings, schema, and local SEO. Leaving it half-done means months of slow recovery.
