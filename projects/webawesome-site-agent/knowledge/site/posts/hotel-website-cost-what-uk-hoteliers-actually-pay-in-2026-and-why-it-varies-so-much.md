# Hotel Website Cost: What UK Hoteliers Actually Pay in 2026 (and Why It Varies So Much)

If you’ve ever typed “hotel website cost” into Google, you’ve probably been hit with answers ranging from £500 to £50,000+ — with no real explanation of what you’re actually getting. That range isn’t helpful. It’s confusing. The truth is, a hotel website isn’t a single product with a fixed price. It’s a stack of decisions, and the cost shifts depending on what your hotel needs, who your guests are, and how direct bookings fit into your wider revenue strategy. Let’s break that down properly. What Actually Drives the Cost of a Hotel Website? The biggest cost driver isn’t design — it’s functionality . A boutique B&B with five rooms and a simple booking flow has very different needs than a 120-room property with a restaurant, spa, events calendar, and multi-language support. Here’s what typically moves the price up or down: Booking engine integration. Connecting to a system like SiteMinder, Cloudbeds, or a custom PMS adds work — and therefore cost. The more properties and room types involved, the more complex it gets. Number of pages and page templates. Rooms, dining, spa, weddings, location, blog, FAQs. Each template type adds design and build time. Photography and content. If you already have professional imagery and copy, that’s a saving. If we need to factor in stock imagery or copywriting, it shifts the scope. Custom features. Live availability, gift voucher purchases, dynamic pricing displays, loyalty sign-ups — all useful, but each one adds development hours. SEO foundation. A genuinely locally optimised site with proper schema, meta structure, and fast load times isn’t a luxury for a hotel — it’s how guests find you on Google in the first place. What You Should Actually Expect to Invest We don’t publish a one-size-fits-all price, because we’d be lying if we did. Our hotel redesign packages typically start in the low-thousands and scale up based on booking integration complexity, room count, and the feature set you need. As a rough guide, if you’re a small independent hotel or guesthouse wanting something clean, fast, mobile-friendly, and connected to a major booking engine, you’re looking at the lower end. If you’re a larger property with multiple integrations, a multilingual site, or a full rebrand to go alongside the build, you’ll be at the higher end — and you should expect that. What’s not worth doing is going for the cheapest quote you can find. A £600 template site will look like a £600 template site, and it won’t convert direct bookings the way you need it to. Every booking you lose to Booking.com because your own site can’t compete is revenue you’re handing over to them, with commission attached. What Separates a Hotel Website That Pays for Itself From One That Doesn’t It’s not the homepage slider. It’s the small details. Mobile performance. A huge share of travel browsing happens on phones, often on patchy hotel Wi-Fi. Your site has to load in under three seconds. Clear booking paths. Guests should never have to hunt for how to check availability. Three taps or fewer — ideally to a real-time calendar, not a contact form. Trust signals that are actually visible. Reviews, awards, star ratings. They belong high on the page, not buried in a footer. Fast, frictionless enquiries. Whether you use a custom enquiry form built for your property or a live chat tool, every step you remove from the booking journey recovers bookings. A Quick Pre-Build Checklist Before you brief any designer or agency, run through these — they save you time, money, and awkward mid-project changes: Do you know your must-have features? Be ruthless — “nice to have” can wait for phase two. Is your booking engine decided? This should be locked in before design starts. Are your room types, photos, and descriptions finalised? “We’ll update it later” is the leading cause of delayed launches. Do you have a clear idea of your guest demographic? A resort hotel and a business-traveller hotel need totally different sites. Have you thought about how the site fits into your wider marketing — SEO, email, social?
