# 7 Signs Your Care Home Website Needs a Redesign (And What to Do About It)

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/7-signs-your-care-home-website-needs-a-redesign-and-what-to-do-about-it/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:11:13

Choosing a care home is one of the most emotionally loaded decisions a family will ever make. Long before anyone walks through your reception, they will have spent hours online comparing options, reading reviews and quietly judging whether your website feels like the kind of place they could trust with their mum or dad. If your site makes that process harder than it needs to be, you are quietly losing enquiries to the care home down the road whose website feels calmer, clearer and more human. Here are seven signs your care home website needs a redesign. 1. Your CQC rating and accreditations are buried or missing Families actively look for your latest CQC rating, your registration details and any sector accreditations. If a visitor has to dig through three pages to find them, or they are not on the site at all, you are creating doubt where you should be building confidence. Put your current CQC rating, latest inspection date and key accreditations near the top of the homepage. Use the actual rating graphic, not just text. 2. It does not work properly on a phone Most families start their research on a phone, often late at night after a difficult conversation. Older relatives visiting your site themselves are frequently using a tablet or phone too. If your text is too small to read without zooming, buttons are hard to tap or images overlap on smaller screens, that experience tells families you are behind the times on the basics. A mobile-first redesign is not optional anymore. 3. Pages take forever to load Slow websites lose enquiries in every sector, and care homes are no exception. Heavy image galleries, outdated plugins and bloated page builders all add up. Aim for your key pages to load in under a few seconds on a normal 4G connection. Stripping back what is on each page is usually the place to start. 4. The design looks tired or generic Web design trends from ten years ago now feel distinctly dated, and outdated visual design subconsciously signals outdated care. A clean, calm, well-spaced layout with high-quality photography and genuinely readable typography will always out-perform a template site that every other home in the area looks identical to. If your site could be mistaken for a website built for any other type of business, it is time for a rethink. 5. Key information is hard to find Families on your site almost always want the same handful of things: fees and what is included, the types of care you offer (residential, dementia, nursing, respite), visiting arrangements, room features, and how to arrange a viewing. If any of these require more than two clicks from the homepage, your information architecture is working against you. Put answers where people expect them, and put the question behind a chat or call if it is genuinely complicated. 6. There is no real sense of the home itself Care home decisions are emotional. Stock photos of smiling seniors and clinical stock photography of corridors do nothing. Invest in proper, recent photography of your real rooms, lounges, gardens, dining areas and staff. A short, well-produced video walk-through can completely change how a family feels about your home before they ever visit. If your current site has neither, this alone could justify a redesign. 7. Your enquiry form is a black hole If you are using a generic contact form that asks for a name, email and a comments box, you are leaving conversion on the table. A care home enquiry form should politely ask the right questions so your team can respond to the right person, in the right way, quickly: who the enquiry is about, the kind of care being considered, a rough timescale, and how they would prefer to be contacted. Well-designed enquiry forms directly translate to more booked viewings. The quiet killer behind all of this: weak local SEO Even a beautiful care home website is wasted effort if families in your area cannot find it. Searches like “care homes near me” and “dementia care in [town]” are increasingly competitive. If your site is not showing up in Google’s local results or on the map pack, your competitors are. A proper care home redesign should always include local SEO foundations built in from day one , not bolted on later. What a good care home website actually does A well-designed care home site reassures quickly, answers the obvious questions without friction, makes it easy to enquire, feels approachable rather than clinical, and ranks for the local searches families actually type. It works just as well on a daughter’s phone at 11pm as it does on a desktop in an office. If several of the signs above sound familiar, a redesign is not a luxury.
