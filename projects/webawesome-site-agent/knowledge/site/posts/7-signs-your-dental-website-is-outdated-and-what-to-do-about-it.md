# 7 Signs Your Dental Website Is Outdated (And What to Do About It)

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/7-signs-your-dental-website-is-outdated-and-what-to-do-about-it/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:12:48

A new patient searching for a dentist will typically look at three or four practice websites before deciding who to call. Your site has roughly ten seconds to earn their trust. If it’s looking tired, slow, or stuck in design trends from half a decade ago, you’re losing bookings to competitors down the road. We’ve built dental websites at WebAwesome, including angeldentalpractice.co.uk . Honestly, the design and build itself is often the simpler part. The harder challenge is what happens after launch: most practice owners simply forget about the site, and it slowly becomes stale. That staleness does more damage than an outdated colour scheme ever could. Here’s how to tell if yours is overdue for attention. 1. You can’t book a single thing online Patients now expect to book, reschedule, or at least request an appointment without picking up the phone. If your only option is “call us between 9 and 5,” you’re adding friction that your competitors don’t have. A modern dental website either integrates directly with your practice management software or links to a clean booking widget that syncs with your real diary. 2. It looks broken on a phone Most web traffic now comes from phones and tablets. If your site pins you to a desktop, squeezes text into microscopic columns, or forces sideways scrolling, visitors leave within seconds. A responsive layout isn’t a nice-to-have anymore, it’s the baseline. 3. There’s no clear sense of who you are A dental website without a friendly “Meet the team” page, real photos of your practice, and a short note about your philosophy feels anonymous. Patients choose people, not buildings. Stock imagery of smiling models in white coats doesn’t build trust, it builds suspicion that something is being hidden. 4. The content hasn’t moved in years This is the big one. It’s the issue we see most often across the dental sites we’ve built. The home page still mentions your old opening hours. The blog stopped in 2022. There’s a “Latest News” section showing events from three years ago. Search engines notice stagnant content and quietly demote you. Patients notice it and assume the practice itself is in decline. A website is not a one-time project you set and forget. It needs the same ongoing care as your patient recall system. If you don’t have time to update it yourself, that’s exactly the kind of thing a small monthly retainer covers. 5. There’s no visible social proof Reviews are the single most persuasive element on a dental website. If your testimonials page has three entries from 2019, or your Google rating isn’t displayed anywhere on the site, you’re leaving conversions on the table. Embedding live Google reviews or Trustpilot scores keeps this section fresh automatically, with almost no effort from you. 6. You’re invisible in local search Try searching “dentist near me” or “[your area] dentist” on your phone right now. If your practice doesn’t appear in the map pack or the first page of organic results, your website is failing at its single most important job. Local SEO is a discipline of its own, but a bare-bones site with no location pages, no schema markup, and no Google Business Profile linkage will never rank competitively. 7. Loading is painfully slow Anything beyond a couple of seconds and people bounce. Uncompressed images, heavy page builders, and unused plugins are the usual culprits. Speed affects both your Google ranking and your conversion rate, so it’s worth running your site through Google’s free PageSpeed Insights tool today. Why a fresh design alone won’t save you It’s tempting to commission a sleek redesign with a new hero banner and trendy fonts, then declare the job done. But design without ongoing content updates, SEO work, and conversion tweaks is just a digital coat of paint. Within a year, you’ll be back exactly where you started today. The practices that win online are the ones treating their website like a living part of the business, not a printed brochure. We’ve written about a closely related problem in our post on signs your church website needs a redesign , and the principles overlap more than you might expect. What to do next If you’ve spotted three or more of the signs above, your site is probably costing you new patients every month. The good news is you don’t necessarily need to start from scratch. A focused redesign of the key pages, a proper booking flow, and a simple plan for keeping content fresh is often enough to turn things around.
