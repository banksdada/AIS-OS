# How Often Should You Redesign Your Freelancer Website? (And When to Push It Sooner)

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/how-often-should-you-redesign-your-freelancer-website-and-when-to-push-it-sooner/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:01:17

If you’re a freelancer, your website is usually the first place a potential client decides whether to trust you with their project. Make it slow, dated, or hard to read on a phone, and they’ll quietly move on to someone else. So how often should you actually redesign it? The short answer Aim for a meaningful redesign roughly every 2–3 years. Smaller updates — new portfolio pieces, refreshed copy, small layout tweaks — should happen continuously, every few months. That’s noticeably faster than the old “every 5 years” rule. A freelancer’s services, niche, and credibility signals shift much more quickly than a corporate brand, and the bar for a credible-looking site has been steadily rising. Why freelancer sites age faster Most big companies redesign once every 4–5 years because their core brand rarely shifts. Freelancers are different: Your services change as skills and demand shift — often within a year or two. Your portfolio needs regular fresh work front and centre. Pricing, process, and positioning evolve as you get more experience. Clients increasingly check your site on mobile first. Design and UX expectations drift every year; even subtle shifts can make a 2023 build feel tired by 2026. If you built your site yourself in a weekend with a template, it probably aged even faster. Signs you should redesign now, not later Skip the calendar and check for these. Three or more of them usually mean it’s time for a proper rebuild: It’s not mobile-first. Most visitors look at sites on their phones first. If yours was designed at a desk and then squeezed down, the experience is probably clunky. Load speed is below average. Slow pages lose visitors fast. A quick run through Google’s PageSpeed Insights will tell you where you stand. Your portfolio is buried. If someone can’t see your best three pieces within a few seconds of landing, they’re gone. No clear next step. A freelancer site without an obvious “book a call” or “send a brief” action is leaving money on the table. A conversion-focused design matters more here than aesthetics — see our take on why functional websites convert, not just beautiful ones . You sound like everyone else. Generic stock photos, vague copy, no clear niche. You hid your prices, process, or contact info. Clients want to qualify themselves out before booking a call. Redesign vs refresh: don’t confuse them A full redesign means a new structure, new layout, often a rebuild from scratch. A refresh is smaller: new headshots, updated services, the latest case study added, copy tightened, maybe a colour tweak. A healthy rhythm looks like: Every 1–2 months: add a new portfolio piece, update testimonials, fix small UX annoyances. Every 12 months: review copy, services, and pricing. Refresh visuals where needed. Every 24–36 months: consider a full redesign — new layout, new template or build, possibly a new platform. This keeps your site from ever feeling visibly outdated, and means no single redesign is ever a massive, painful project. What to prioritise in a freelancer redesign When you do bite the bullet and rebuild, focus on what actually wins clients: A clear above-the-fold pitch. Who you serve, what you do, why it matters. Five seconds or less. Portfolio with context. Not just pretty images — short notes on the problem, what you did, and the outcome. A frictionless enquiry path. A simple form or a clear booking link — both work. Getting your layout to guide users to act is where friendly design really pays off: see how user-friendly layouts benefit your business . Proof signals. Testimonials, logos of past clients, certifications — anything credible. Honest pricing or a “starting from” line. Hiding everything generates extra sales calls with people who can’t afford you. Mobile performance treated as a first-class concern. Not something you check at the end. Spend money here, not on animated hero sections or trendy gradients. A small note on cost For most freelancers, a considered redesign every couple of years is comfortably within a sensible budget — especially compared to the revenue of even one new client it brings in. If your current site is a DIY build you’re embarrassed to send people to, that’s usually the single biggest barrier to growth you’re carrying, and it’s quietly costing you enquiries every month.
