# How Often Should You Redesign Your Law Firm Website? A Practical Guide

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/how-often-should-you-redesign-your-law-firm-website-a-practical-guide/
Updated: 2026-10-26T05:41:40

If you run a UK law firm, your website is one of your most important client acquisition channels. But like any tool, it ages. The question isn’t whether your site will need a redesign — it will. The question is how often, and how to tell when the time is right. The short answer: every 2–3 years, with smaller updates in between There’s no single legal industry rule, but for most UK law firms, a full website redesign should happen roughly every two to three years. Between full redesigns, you should be making smaller updates — copy, imagery, plugins, security patches, content — every few months. If your site hasn’t been touched in four or more years, it’s effectively out of date. Browser standards, mobile expectations, Google ranking factors and client behaviour have all moved on whether you have or not. Why law firm websites go stale faster than most Legal websites aren’t brochures. They’re working tools for client acquisition, and the environment around them shifts quickly: Mobile traffic now dominates. The majority of website visits across nearly every sector come from phones. If your site wasn’t designed mobile-first, it loses readers, enquiries and Google rankings. Google’s ranking factors change constantly. Core Web Vitals, page experience signals and E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authority, trust) all carry real weight. A site that was technically solid three years ago may be quietly underperforming now. Client expectations have risen. People searching for a solicitor expect a fast, modern, trustworthy experience. An outdated design reads as “outdated practice,” whether that’s fair or not. Competition isn’t standing still. Competing firms in your practice area and city are investing in local SEO , faster sites and sharper content. Yesterday’s “good enough” is tomorrow’s behind. Signs it’s time for a redesign (not just a tweak) Some issues can be solved with small fixes. Others mean you need a full rebuild. Here’s how to tell: Your site isn’t mobile-responsive — or it technically is, but the layout is awkward on phones. This alone can justify a redesign. Page load is slow. Anything over three seconds on mobile loses a significant chunk of visitors before they see a word. You’re not getting enquiry form submissions. If your site looks polished but doesn’t convert , the structure is failing you. Your branding has changed — new logo, new practice areas, merger, rebrand. You can’t easily update content yourself. If every small change requires a developer, your CMS is working against you. Google Search Console shows declining clicks or rankings. Often a sign the technical foundation has aged out. What to refresh every year (without a full redesign) A redesign doesn’t mean starting from scratch every time. Schedule these annually: Update team bios, photos and practice area pages. Refresh case studies, testimonials and trust signals. Add new content targeting the searches your clients actually make. Our guide to proven SEO strategies is a good starting point. Review analytics: which pages convert, which don’t. Run a basic accessibility and speed audit. What a redesign actually involves for a law firm For a typical small to mid-sized UK firm, a proper redesign isn’t just a facelift. It usually covers: Strategy: clear positioning, services and ideal client profiles. Information architecture: how pages are grouped and linked. Design: modern, credible, mobile-first, on-brand. Copy: rewritten for clarity, search intent and conversion — not just “what we do.” Technical foundations: hosting, security, speed, schema markup, contact forms, analytics. Compliance: GDPR, cookie consent and accessibility basics. A competent agency should walk you through each of these, not just hand you a pretty mock-up. Cost, timeline and how to think about ROI A professional law firm website redesign in the UK typically takes six to twelve weeks and ranges from mid four-figures for a small firm to low five-figures for something bespoke with custom integrations. The honest way to think about ROI: a single new client case for many UK legal services covers the cost of the website several times over. The site isn’t an expense line — it’s a client acquisition channel.
