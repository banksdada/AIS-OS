# The Small Business Website Redesign Checklist: 13 Things to Get Right Before You Launch

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/the-small-business-website-redesign-checklist-13-things-to-get-right-before-you-launch/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:07:58

A website redesign is one of the biggest investments a small business can make. Done well, it pays back in better leads, more sales, and fewer “we can’t find anything” support queries. Done poorly, it’s weeks of disruption and a site that still doesn’t convert. Most small businesses we work with come to us after a redesign that didn’t deliver — usually because the project skipped a few essential steps. Use the checklist below to make sure yours doesn’t. Before You Start 1. Define what success looks like. “New website” isn’t a goal. Is it more quote requests? More phone calls from your local area? More online bookings? Write down the 1–3 conversions that actually pay your bills. Every design decision should serve one of them. 2. Audit your current site honestly. Pull the data: top pages, bounce rate, mobile traffic share, current leads per month, and the questions customers ask your team again and again. Those repeated questions are signposts pointing to gaps in your current site. 3. Set a realistic budget and timeline. A proper small business redesign typically runs 6–10 weeks from kickoff to launch, not counting content writing. If anyone promises it in two weeks while also building custom features, something will get cut. Design and User Experience 4. Plan mobile-first, not mobile-last. Most of your visitors are on phones. Sketch the mobile layout first, then adapt for desktop — not the other way around. Test on a real phone, in real light, on a real train commute. 5. Simplify your navigation ruthlessly. If a first-time visitor can’t tell what you do within five seconds, the menu is too complex. Aim for 5–7 top-level items max, with clear labels written in your customer’s language, not your internal jargon. 6. Put the right call-to-action above the fold. Each main page should have one primary action — “Get a quote,” “Book a consultation,” “Call us now” — visible without scrolling. Adding three competing buttons doesn’t give visitors options; it confuses them. 7. Build trust visibly. Reviews, accreditations, client logos, photos of your actual team (not stock imagery), and a clear About page all do heavy lifting. For local businesses, most of these trust signals feed directly into local search visibility — our guide on dominating your local market walks through how to stack them properly. Technical Foundations 8. Get page speed under control. Aim for a Largest Contentful Paint of under 2.5 seconds on mobile. Heavy stock video backgrounds, oversized hero images, and bloated plugins are the usual suspects. Test with Google’s PageSpeed Insights before launch. 9. Make it accessible. Colour contrast, keyboard navigation, proper heading order, alt text for images, and captioned video aren’t extras — they’re the baseline. Accessible sites tend to rank better, and they don’t lock out a meaningful slice of your audience who rely on them. 10. Pick the right type of website. Not every small business needs a bespoke build. As we outline in our breakdown of the three main types of websites , for most small businesses under around 50 staff, a well-built template platform hits the sweet spot of cost, speed, and flexibility. Content and SEO 11. Plan your SEO basics before launch. Page titles, meta descriptions, header tags, internal linking, and compressed images all need to be in place from day one. SEO isn’t a bolt-on. It’s worth treating as a lifetime event, not a one-time event — the sites that steadily outrank competitors are the ones that keep updating. 12. Write the copy before the build, not after. The most common redesign mistake we see is designing first and then scrabbling to decide what goes on the page. Decide the message, then design around it. After Launch 13. Plan for the first 90 days. Set up Google Search Console and GA4 from day one. Watch the data weekly for the first month, monthly after that. Update content based on what real visitors are actually searching for and doing. Two Common Mistakes to Avoid Designing for your team, not your customer. Internal preferences lose to user behaviour, every time. Treating launch as the finish line. A site that isn’t updated and improved steadily will quietly decay in search rankings within months. The Bottom Line A small business website redesign isn’t really about the website — it’s about removing friction between a curious visitor and a paying customer.
