# Do you have a Clear AI Strategy for Your Coaching Business

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/do-you-have-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-coaching-business/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:37:14

Whether you run a one-person coaching practice or lead a team of coaches, AI has shifted from “someday maybe” to “this quarter’s decision.” The trouble isn’t finding tools – there are plenty. It’s knowing which ones actually move the needle, and how to wire them into the business without creating a mess of subscriptions that nobody uses. After working with coaches and consultancy founders on their websites and digital tools, we’ve seen what separates a plan that sticks from one that fizzles out after a flashy demo. Here’s a practical five-step framework for building an AI strategy that actually works for a coaching business. Step 1: Audit where your time and money actually go Before you pick a single tool, get honest about the bottlenecks. Pull the last 30 days and write down the tasks that eat the most hours: client onboarding forms, scheduling, session prep, content creation, follow-up emails, invoicing, proposal writing. Most coaches we work with are surprised at how much sits in admin rather than coaching itself. Goal: a one-page list of the 5-10 activities that consume the most time per week. Anything repetitive and rules-based is a candidate for AI. Anything that needs your lived experience and empathy isn’t. Step 2: Pick the wins that pay back fast A clear AI strategy doesn’t try to automate everything at once. Start with tasks that are high frequency , rules-based , and low risk if imperfect . Examples that fit coaching businesses well: Drafting session recaps and follow-up emails Generating first-pass content ideas for blogs, newsletters, social posts Summarising discovery call transcripts Building reusable proposal and welcome pack templates Skip the heavy creative judgement – keep that with you or your team. The point of step 2 is to build momentum with quick wins, not chase moonshots. Step 3: Map the client journey your website powers This is where most coaches’ strategies quietly fall apart. AI lives somewhere – a Notion doc, a ChatGPT tab, a Loom – but it isn’t wired into the place clients actually meet you: your website. Walk through the client journey from first visit to repeat booking and ask, at each step, “Where could AI help here?” Common friction points we’ve helped coaching clients rebuild include: A smarter enquiry form that pre-qualifies and routes the lead An onboarding flow that adapts questions based on the client’s stated goal A resource library that recommends the next article or module based on what they’ve already read Automatic booking reminders with a short, AI-generated check-in message If your site is still acting like a static brochure, the AI you bolt on later will feel bolted on. Get the journey mapped first, then choose the tools. Step 4: Decide what to build, buy, or skip For most coaching businesses, the answer is mostly buy, sparingly build, and consciously skip. Off-the-shelf tools (Calendly, Notion AI, Otter, ChatGPT Team, Make, Zapier) cover the bulk of what you need without custom code. Where it makes sense to build: When the AI needs to read or write to your own client database When the experience has to feel native to your brand – for example, an AI intake assistant sitting on your own site When client data privacy rules require it This is the point where a good web partner earns their keep. A bespoke intake widget, a branded chatbot, an AI quiz that recommends a programme – these are website or app features, not off-the-shelf AI tools. Step 5: Pilot, measure, then expand Pick one or two uses from step 2, wire them into your website in step 4, and run them for 4-6 weeks. Measure them against the original bottleneck: hours saved, leads captured, response times, conversion to booked call. If the numbers move, expand. If they don’t, kill it and try the next candidate. Coaches who treat AI like a habit – small, repeated, measured – end up with something usable. Coaches who treat it like a project with a launch date end up with unused subscriptions and a folder of half-set-up tools. Where to start this week If you’ve read this far, you already know more than most of your peers. The single most useful thing you can do today is the step 1 audit. Block 90 minutes, list the ten tasks that consume your week, and circle the three most repetitive. That list becomes your AI roadmap for the next quarter.
