# 7 Signs Your Church Website Needs a Redesign (And How to Fix Them)

If your church website still looks like it was built in 2012 and last edited in 2019, you’re not alone. Across the UK, hundreds of churches are running sites that quietly cost them donations, attendees, and connection with their community. Most weren’t neglected on purpose — they were built by a well-meaning volunteer, then handed off to whoever had five minutes spare. Years later, nobody owns it. We’re WebAwesome , a London-based web design consultancy. We’ve redesigned three church and community organisation sites in the past couple of years, and the issues we see are remarkably consistent. Here’s what to look for, and what to do about it. 1. Your donation flow is clunky or hidden This is the big one. If someone has to click three times, scroll past a sermon archive, and fill in a 12-field form to give £10, most of them won’t bother. We’ve seen churches lose measurable giving simply because the “Donate” button was buried in the footer or linked to a generic PayPal page with no branding. A good church donation flow is visible on every page, mobile-optimised, and takes under a minute to complete. Gift Aid should be opt-in by default, not opt-out. 2. Service times aren’t on the homepage The single most common reason someone visits a church website is to check what time is Sunday service? If that answer isn’t visible above the fold on mobile, you’ve already lost them — possibly for forever, because they’ll go to the church down the road whose website answered instantly. Put the next service time, address, and a one-tap “Get Directions” link in the most prominent position on the page. 3. There’s no mobile-friendly calendar People — especially younger families and visitors — need to know what’s coming up. Bible studies, youth groups, community events, fundraising dinners. If your events calendar only works on desktop, doesn’t show upcoming events first, or requires users to click into each one for a date, it’s not doing its job. The fix: a clean, mobile-first calendar that lists the next 4–6 events with dates, times, and a single click for full details. 4. The site fails basic accessibility checks This matters for any organisation, but for a church it’s particularly important — your congregation likely includes older members, people with visual impairments, and visitors using assistive tech. Common issues we see: Text that’s too small or low-contrast to read Images with no alt text (so screen readers say “image, image, image”) Sermon videos with no captions Forms that can’t be completed with a keyboard Most of this is fixable in a redesign without breaking the bank. We covered the broader principles in our guide on user-friendly website layouts . 5. Nobody knows who currently maintains the site “Oh, Dave used to do it, but he moved to Northampton.” If that’s the answer when you ask who can update your site, it’s a red flag. Modern church sites should be editable by any reasonably confident volunteer — no FTP, no HTML, no plugins that break every time there’s an update. We hand over sites with a short, plain-English guide so your admin team (who are volunteers with day jobs) can confidently make changes without phoning us. 6. It loads slowly — especially on the vicar’s phone If your site takes more than a few seconds to load on a typical UK mobile connection, roughly half your visitors are gone before they see anything. Bloated slideshows, uncompressed images from 2017, and ten tracking scripts are the usual suspects. A lean redesign with properly sized images and modern hosting typically cuts load time dramatically without losing any meaningful content. 7. There’s no clear path for a first-time visitor Regular members know your church. A first-time visitor doesn’t. They want to know: What should I expect? Is there a dress code? Will my kids be looked after? Where do I park? If your site makes a newcomer hunt through five pages to answer these, they’ll often just not come. Build a dedicated “Visiting for the first time?” page that answers the obvious questions in plain language. It costs almost nothing and dramatically improves how welcoming your church feels online.
