# 7 Signs Your Homecare Website Needs a Redesign (And What to Do About It)

Why Homecare Websites Often Fall Behind Homecare is built on trust. Families searching for care for an ageing parent or a vulnerable relative need to find you quickly, understand what you offer, and feel confident you are the right choice — often within minutes, on a phone, in a moment of stress. Yet here is the challenge we hear again and again from UK homecare businesses we have worked with: there is rarely a dedicated budget for continually updating and modernising the website to keep pace with how users actually search, browse, and enquire today. The site gets built, life gets busy, and two or three years later it quietly stops doing its job. If that sounds familiar, here are seven signs your homecare website probably needs a redesign. 1. Your Site Does Not Work Properly on a Phone The majority of care-related searches now happen on mobile devices. If your site pinches, zooms, or sends users sideways to scroll through long blocks of text, you are losing enquiries before anyone has read a word. A modern redesign will lean mobile-first — clear menus, tappable phone numbers, and forms that are easy to fill out one-handed. 2. Families Cannot Quickly Find What They Need Visitors usually want to know three things fast: which services you offer, where you cover, and how to get in touch. If those answers are buried two or three clicks deep, people leave. A redesign reorganises your navigation around what your visitors actually need — not around your internal team structure. 3. There Are No Clear Trust Signals In homecare, credibility is everything. If your homepage does not prominently show your CQC rating, accreditations, insurer details, safeguarding policies, or verified reviews, families will quietly move on to a competitor who does. We regularly audit homecare sites where this crucial layer of reassurance is missing. 4. Your Enquiry Process Is Friction-Heavy If someone has to email, then call, then fill in a form, then wait for a callback, you have created an unnecessary hurdle at the exact moment motivation is highest. Modern homecare sites let visitors choose: a short form, a tap-to-call button, an online booking widget, or an instant live chat. The more options you offer, the more enquiries you will receive. 5. The Site Looks and Feels Outdated Design trends are not vanity — they signal whether a business is current, active, and attentive to detail. If your site still uses a PDF download as a homepage banner, stock photos from years ago, or fonts that feel clunky, that is the impression of your care you will be giving too. A refresh is also a chance to use real photos of your actual carers and clients (with proper consent), which builds far more warmth than any stock image ever will. 6. You Are Invisible in Local Search If you Google ‘homecare [your town]’ and your competitors — not you — occupy the map pack and top organic results, your website is not pulling its weight locally. A redesign paired with local SEO improvements can put you back in front of the families actively searching . Fresh content, location pages, and proper schema markup make a real difference here. 7. You Are Paying for a Site You Cannot Update One of the most common frustrations we hear: the original agency built the site, will not hand over full access, or charges every time a single date or photo needs changing. A redesign should put you back in control, with a CMS you can actually use without picking up the phone. This is also where ongoing updating becomes affordable — the recurring cost the sector struggles with is largely solved when the site is built right in the first place. The Real Cost of Doing Nothing It is tempting to push the redesign back another year. But every month your outdated site sits there, it is fielding fewer enquiries, ranking worse against newer competitors, and quietly undermining the professionalism of the care you actually deliver. Families do not separate the website from the service — to them, it is the service, at least at first glance. The good news is that a redesign does not have to be expensive.
