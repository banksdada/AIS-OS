# Law Firm Website Redesign Checklist: 12 Essentials UK Firms Can’t Afford to Miss

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/law-firm-website-redesign-checklist-12-essentials-uk-firms-cant-afford-to-miss/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:06:07

If your firm’s website still looks like it did before the pandemic, you’re leaving enquiries on the table. In a sector where trust is everything and most prospective clients comparison-shop online before they ever pick up the phone, your website isn’t a brochure anymore — it’s your first impression, your receptionist, and your 24/7 sales tool. This checklist walks you through the essentials of a high-performing law firm website in 2026. Use it whether you’re scoping a full redesign or a focused refresh. 1. Lead With Trust, Not Just Branding A law firm’s homepage has roughly three seconds to convince a visitor you are credible. That means visible Solicitor Regulation Authority registration, Law Society badges, and any relevant accreditations placed above the fold. Real photographs of your actual team (not stock imagery of gavel-wielding lawyers) and prominent client reviews or case outcomes build credibility fast. As we put it in our piece on functional versus beautiful websites , design that doesn’t serve the visitor’s decision-making process is just decoration. 2. Make Practice Areas Scannable Most visitors arrive looking for a specific service — conveyancing, employment, family, commercial disputes. Bury those pages behind vague “Our Services” menus and you’ll lose them. Each practice area should have its own dedicated page with a short description, the problems you solve, and an obvious next step. 3. Honest, Searchable Attorney Bios People hire people, especially in legal services. Every solicitor should have a profile page with: Qualifications and year of qualification Specialisms in plain English An approachable professional photograph Direct contact details and ideally a short video introduction 4. Treat Local SEO as a Core Channel “Solicitor near me” searches remain some of the most valuable traffic a firm can attract. Your redesign should bake in a consistent Name, Address and Phone (NAP) across every page and listing, location-specific landing pages for each office, and Google Business Profile integration. Our guide on local SEO for small businesses goes deeper on the tactics, but the principle is simple: if your firm doesn’t show up in local results, your competitors’ intake teams will be picking up the call instead. 5. Mobile Experience Comes First A large and growing share of legal searches now happen on phones. Mobile isn’t a secondary version of your site; it’s the primary one. Test your redesign on real devices, not just resized browser windows, and make sure tap targets — the phone number, the consultation form — are thumb-friendly. 6. Speed and Core Web Vitals Slow sites kill conversions. Aim for sub-two-second loads on mobile. Compress assets, lazy-load images below the fold, and audit third-party scripts (chat widgets, scheduling tools) that often drag performance down without anyone noticing. 7. Frictionless Consultation Bookings If a visitor has to dig for a contact form or download a PDF to book a call, you’ve already lost most of them. Include a persistent “Book a Consultation” or “Call Now” call-to-action in the header, a short multi-step form that doesn’t ask for a full case history upfront, and real-time booking tools where appropriate. The faster you turn intent into a recorded enquiry, the better your conversion rate will be. 8. GDPR and SRA-Compliant Data Capture As a regulated firm, you cannot afford sloppy data handling. Your forms must carry clear consent checkboxes for marketing communications, link to a current privacy notice and cookie policy, securely transmit everything over HTTPS, and document the lawful basis for processing enquirer data. The Information Commissioner’s Office is not a regulator you want to learn about the hard way. 9. Trust Signals Throughout the Journey Accreditations, awards, professional memberships, anonymised case outcomes, testimonials and press mentions should be sprinkled across the site, not just the homepage. Many firms have impressive achievements they simply haven’t surfaced online — a redesign is the perfect moment to fix that. 10. Accessibility That Opens Your Client Base WCAG-compliant design is both a legal and ethical obligation under the Equality Act 2010, and a practical one given the demographics you may serve. Use proper heading hierarchies, alt text for images, sufficient colour contrast, and keyboard-friendly navigation. Accessible sites also tend to perform better in search. 11. Structured Data and Schema Markup LegalService and LocalBusiness schema help search engines understand what you do and where you operate, which can support richer results on Google. It’s a relatively cheap technical win that most smaller firms overlook entirely. 12. Analytics Worth Reading Don’t launch a redesign without measurement. Set up goal tracking for consultation requests, phone calls and form completions, and review the numbers quarterly. A site that’s not being measured is one that’s quietly leaking you revenue. Common Pitfalls to Avoid Designing for the managing partner’s preferences rather than the client’s Skipping a content audit and dragging old, off-brand pages into a new template Going live without redirects and breaking years of SEO equity Treating the redesign as a one-off instead of the start of an ongoing programme A well-executed redesign typically pays for itself many times over through improved enquiry conversion and reduced marketing spend.
