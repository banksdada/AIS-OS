# 5 Steps to a Clear AI Strategy for Your Consultancy Business

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/5-steps-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-consultancy-business/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:38:56

Most consultancies we speak to in 2026 are stuck in the same place. Everyone agrees AI matters. Nobody has a coherent plan. Tools are being trialled by individuals behind the scenes, there’s no governance, and when leadership asks “what are we actually doing with AI?” the honest answer is a shrug. This post shares a focused 5-step framework to fix that. No hype, no 50-page strategy deck. Just a practical path from scattered experimentation to deliberate, measurable adoption. Step 1: Audit where time and margin actually leak Before picking tools, map where your consultants spend hours that don’t earn fees. The usual suspects: Drafting proposals, statements of work, and repeat deliverables Research synthesis and data wrangling Meeting notes, follow-ups, and internal status reports Onboarding docs and internal knowledge lookup Ask your team to log a week of frustrating manual work. Patterns usually jump out within days — and that becomes your shortlist. Step 2: Pick 2–3 use cases, not 20 The biggest mistake we see is trying to “do AI across the business” in one go. It never sticks. Pick two or three use cases that tick three boxes: High volume — done often across the team Low judgment — mostly templated or pattern-based Clear upside — measurable time or margin gain Strong starting points for consultancies include proposal drafting, market research summaries, internal knowledge retrieval, and first-pass client diagnostics. Pick the two or three that match your audit — not the ones your partners read about in a newsletter. Step 3: Decide what to buy vs what to build You almost never need a bespoke AI product in step one. Most consultancies get the bulk of the upside from well-configured off-the-shelf tools plus changed workflows. Worth evaluating early: ChatGPT Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot, Claude for Teams, and Notion AI for internal use Vertical tools for specific disciplines — legal, financial analysis, product strategy Only move to custom builds when you’ve hit genuine limits of off-the-shelf and the use case justifies the investment. Your wider digital infrastructure matters here too — if your client portal, internal knowledge base, or website is holding back how your team works with AI, that’s worth fixing in parallel. Our business website redesign checklist covers the kind of foundations that make tool rollout smoother. Step 4: Set the rules before you scale AI in a consultancy without governance is a liability waiting to happen. Get clarity on four things: What client data can and can’t go into prompts How AI-generated content is reviewed before it leaves the building Who approves new use cases internally What you tell clients when AI has been used in their deliverable Two hours writing a one-page policy beats six months cleaning up after a breach or a reputation hit. This is also where your website and client-facing comms need to align — clients are increasingly asking, and the wrong answer is worse than no answer. Step 5: Pilot, measure, then scale Run a six-to-eight-week pilot on one team, one client type, and one use case. Decide your metrics before you start: Hours saved per deliverable Brief-to-draft turnaround time Margin per engagement Quality score from a sample reviewer If the numbers don’t beat the baseline, kill the use case and try the next one. If they do, you’ve got a defensible case to roll out — and a story to tell the leaders who’ve been waiting for proof. The bigger picture A clear AI strategy isn’t really about AI. It’s about being deliberate about how your consultancy uses its most expensive resource — your people’s time — and how your digital infrastructure supports them. The consultancies pulling ahead in 2026 aren’t the ones with the most tools plugged in. They’re the ones who chose two or three, embedded them properly, and built the right supporting systems around them — websites, portals, internal tools — that actually let the AI work land.
