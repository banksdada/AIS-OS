# 7 Signs Your Salon Website Needs a Redesign (And What to Do About It)

Updated: 2026-11-02T14:07:09

Your salon website works harder than you might think. It runs 24/7, answers questions while you colour someone’s hair, and quietly convinces strangers on their sofa to book an appointment. When it works, you barely notice it. When it doesn’t, you feel it — in the diary, and in the revenue. If you’ve been meaning to “get around to” a redesign for more than six months, the signs below usually mean it’s overdue. We’ve worked with salons, barbers, and beauty studios across the UK, and these are the seven issues we see most often. 1. There’s no real online booking — just a phone number Asking potential clients to pick up the phone during working hours is leaving money on the table. People browse salons in the evenings, on the bus, between meetings. If your only call to action is “call us to book,” you’re filtering out every client who wants to book in under a minute without talking to anyone. A modern salon website should integrate with a real booking platform (Timely, Fresha, Treatwell, Phorest, SalonIQ — whatever fits your stack). The booking button should sit in the header, not buried three pages deep. 2. It still looks like 2016 Web design trends shift, and so do expectations. If your site uses carousels that auto-rotate, a stock-photo header, or fonts that feel dated, visitors subconsciously equate that with “this place might be past its prime too.” You don’t need anything radical. Clean typography, lots of white space, real photos of your actual salon and your actual team. That’s usually enough to feel current. 3. Service menus and prices are buried People want to know, fast: what does a cut and colour cost, do you do kids, how long does it take? If they can’t answer those three questions in ten seconds, they’ll go to the next salon that lets them. Make price lists visible. Group services logically (cutting, colour, treatments). Don’t hide pricing behind “contact us for a quote” unless there’s genuinely no fixed price — because for almost every salon service, there is. 4. Your team page is a list of generic photos Salons are personal. Clients pick a stylist, not just a salon. Your team page should have real names, real photos, specialisms, and ideally a “book with” button on each profile. If yours is six stock images of smiling women with no names, you’re missing one of your biggest trust-building opportunities. 5. The site is slow or clunky on a phone The majority of salon browsing now happens on mobile. If your site takes more than a few seconds to load, or if the menu is fiddly, you’re losing people before they see anything good. Google also uses mobile performance as a ranking factor for local search — so a slow site hurts you twice. This is one of the highest-ROI fixes. If the rest of your site is fine but slow, a performance pass can lift bookings noticeably on its own. 6. You’re invisible on Google for “salon near me” searches If you type “hairdresser in [your town]” and don’t show up in the local pack (the map and three listings), your website and your Google Business Profile aren’t working together like they should. A redesign is a good moment to sort this out: proper headings, schema markup, location pages if you have multiple branches, and a Google Business Profile that actually matches the site. 7. There are no reviews, no proof, nothing reassuring People are nervous about trying a new salon. Reviews, before/after photos, “as seen in” badges, awards, qualifications — these all lower the barrier. If your site has none of this, and only a generic “welcome to our salon” intro, visitors leave uncertain. Pull in your Google reviews automatically, feature your best transformations in a gallery, and make your qualifications and insurance visible somewhere. Trust is what converts a browser into a booking. What to do if this list feels a bit too familiar You don’t need to overhaul everything at once. The most useful first step is to figure out which of these problems is actually costing you bookings — because that’s the one to fix first. A quick audit of your current site against a small business website redesign checklist will tell you where the biggest gaps are. And if your salon is part of a chain or franchise, the same logic that applies to dental practices and other appointment-led local services applies to you: the website isn’t a brochure, it’s a booking engine.
