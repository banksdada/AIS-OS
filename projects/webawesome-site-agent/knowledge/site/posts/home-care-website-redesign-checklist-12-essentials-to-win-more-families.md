# Home Care Website Redesign Checklist: 12 Essentials to Win More Families

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/home-care-website-redesign-checklist-12-essentials-to-win-more-families/
Updated: 2026-10-25T23:05:25

Home care is one of the most trust-driven purchases a family will ever make. When someone’s looking for care for an elderly parent, a partner recovering from illness, or a relative with a disability, they don’t browse casually. They search, they read, they compare — and they pick the provider that feels safe, professional and easy to deal with. That decision almost always starts on your website. If your site feels dated, slow or full of jargon, families bounce. If it loads quickly, reads clearly, and answers their questions in under a minute, you’re in the conversation. This checklist covers what actually matters when redesigning a home care website, drawn from working with UK care providers and seeing what separates the sites that generate enquiries from the ones that quietly lose them. 1. Trust signals above the fold The top of your homepage has roughly three seconds to answer “can I trust these people?” Make sure visitors can see your CQC rating (where applicable), registration badges, and a genuine photo of your team — not stock imagery. Real photos of caregivers, the office, and the management team Visible CQC rating or equivalent accreditation A short, plain-English statement of who you are and where you cover 2. Plain-English service pages Each core service (domiciliary care, live-in care, dementia support, respite care, end-of-life care) deserves its own page that explains what’s included, who it’s for, and how to arrange it. Avoid clinical language. Write the way a worried daughter or son would actually Google it. 3. Clear pricing or “starting from” indicators Families specifically search for cost. If you can’t publish rates, publish indicative hourly bands or a clear “we’ll quote after a free assessment” path. Hiding pricing entirely is the surest way to lose warm leads. 4. Caregiver profiles that feel human Care is delivered by people. Add real bios: training, years of experience, languages spoken, specialisms. Families want to know who will walk through their parent’s door. 5. Frictionless enquiry forms The “request a callback” or “arrange a home visit” form should ask for no more than 5 fields — name, phone, relation to the person needing care, postcode, and a short note. Long forms kill conversions, and as we cover in our piece on functional websites , every extra field is a small leak in your enquiry pipeline. 6. Multiple contact routes Not everyone wants to fill in a form. Show a visible phone number, a WhatsApp link, and an email address. Older family members typically want to speak to a real person. 7. Reviews and family testimonials Add written testimonials with first name and area (not anonymous praise) and, where you have permission, short video clips. Third-party reviews from Google or Homecare.co.uk can also be embedded. 8. Local SEO foundations Home care is a local business. Your site needs dedicated pages for each area you cover — “Domiciliary care in Bromley”, “Live-in care in Essex” — with unique copy, not just swapped place names. Pair this with a properly optimised Google Business Profile. Our guide to local SEO for small businesses walks through the full approach. 9. Accessibility by default Many of your users are older adults themselves, or family members with impaired vision. Use a readable base font size (17–18px minimum), strong colour contrast, and avoid walls of text. Buttons should be large and tap-friendly on mobile. 10. Fast, mobile-first performance Most searches for care happen on a phone, often in a moment of urgency. If your site takes more than a few seconds to load on 4G, you’re losing enquiries you didn’t know existed. Aim for a tight, modern build with compressed images and minimal plugins. 11. Compliance and policies in human terms Safeguarding, complaints procedure, data protection, privacy — all required, but often buried in legalese. Surface them in plain English and link from the footer. 12. Proper analytics and enquiry tracking You can’t improve what you can’t measure. Make sure the new site has Google Analytics (or a privacy-friendly equivalent), Search Console, and call tracking wired up from day one. Otherwise you’ll redesign again in two years without knowing why. Putting it to work You don’t need every item perfect on day one. But aim to tick at least 9–10 before launch.
