# Some Steps to a Clear AI Strategy for Your Dental Practice

Source: https://www.webawesome.co.uk/some-steps-to-a-clear-ai-strategy-for-your-dental-practice/
Updated: 2026-11-12T09:23:01

Every dental group and solo practice owner we speak to in 2026 is being sold AI. Chatbots for the website. AI voice agents for the phone. Automated recalls. Treatment plan generators. Imaging analysis. The list never ends. What’s missing from most of those conversations is the question that actually matters: what problem are you trying to solve? Tools without a strategy cost money, confuse your team, and rarely deliver the time savings or revenue lift the salespeople promised. Here are five practical steps to put together a clear AI strategy for your dental practice, one that fits how your practice actually runs. Step 1: Map the friction before you map the tools Before you look at a single product, write down the five biggest friction points in your business today. Be honest. Are 30% of inbound calls going to voicemail and never being called back? Is your reception team drowning in recall reminders? Are new patient enquiries coming in through the website but sitting in a shared inbox for days? Is the practice manager spending hours a week on rotas, reports, or stock? Are patients dropping off between consultation and treatment plan acceptance? These are the problems AI can actually help with. A tool bought to chase a trend usually sits unused after three months. A tool bought to fix a named problem gets adopted, measured, and paid back. If your website is part of that friction, it’s worth a quick audit first. We wrote about the most common signs your dental website is outdated in a separate post. Step 2: Decide which jobs AI should do, and which it shouldn’t AI is brilliant at a handful of things and mediocre at a long list. For dental practices, the strongest use cases in 2026 are: Front desk load: answering common questions on the website 24/7, qualifying new patient enquiries, booking routine appointments into your diary. Recall and reactivation: sending personalised reminders, following up on lapsed patients, filling last-minute cancellations. Communication: drafting treatment plan summaries, replying to routine patient messages, generating post-appointment instructions. Back office: transcribing notes, summarising long emails, drafting rotas and reports. What AI still shouldn’t do without human review: clinical diagnosis, treatment planning decisions, anything involving safeguarding, and any patient communication that involves a complaint or a money conversation. Those still need a human, every time. The clearer you are about scope, the easier the rest of the strategy becomes. Step 3: Start with one workflow, not five The single biggest reason AI projects fail in dental practices is trying to do everything at once. Pick one workflow. The best candidates share three traits: High volume: it happens dozens of times a day or week. Low complexity: it follows a recognisable pattern. Easy to measure: you can tell quickly whether it’s working. For most practices, the right first workflow is either website lead capture or recall reactivation. Both check all three boxes and both have a clear revenue line attached. Run it for 60 to 90 days. Measure. Then decide what to add next. Step 4: Get your data and your website ready AI tools are only as good as what they can read. Before you switch anything on, make sure: Your website is up to date, fast, and clearly explains your services and fees. If it isn’t, AI-driven traffic and enquiries will get wasted on a site that doesn’t convert. Our dentist website redesign guide walks through how to fix this. Your treatment list, FAQs, and pricing structure are written down somewhere usable. AI tools need this content to give accurate answers. Your PMS data is clean and consistent. Duplicate patient records and inconsistent treatment codes will sabotage any automation. You have a written policy for what staff and patients are told about AI use, especially when it touches patient communication. This is unglamorous work, but it’s the difference between an AI rollout that saves ten hours a week and one that creates new headaches. Step 5: Measure outcomes, not activity Vanity metrics will eat your budget if you let them. “We got 4,000 chatbot conversations” means nothing. Track instead: Booked appointments generated through AI-driven channels. Response time on new patient enquiries, before and after. Recall reactivation rate and revenue recovered from lapsed patients. Hours saved per week on specific tasks, not in general. Patient satisfaction , ideally checked with a short survey. Review these monthly. Cut anything that isn’t moving the right numbers after 90 days. Add the next workflow only when the current one is genuinely working.
