# Daily content bank — WebAwesome + mentoringba

Built 2026-10-21. **30 days, one post a day, both pages.** Every entry is
copy-paste ready. Voice matched to the AGENTS.md samples — short lines, plain
talk, self-aware, no corporate gloss.

Read the "How to use this" section before posting, and the **Research appendix**
before quoting any number. These are drafts; the AGENTS.md rule stands —
nothing on external content without your review.

---

## How to use this

**Cadence.** One post a day. Alternate pages so neither audience gets two in a
row: WA = webawesome.co.uk, MBA = mentoringba.

**Pillars.** Every post belongs to a pillar. Rotate them rather than posting
your strongest angle five days in a row. The pillars:

| Pillar | What it does |
|---|---|
| PROOF | A real problem you solved. Builds trust. |
| OPINION | A position on AI / automation / the industry. Builds reach. |
| JOURNEY | Build-in-public. Builds a following between wins. |
| TEACH | One practical lesson. Saves and shares. |
| OFFER | Points at what you do. Rare — used sparingly so it doesn't read as selling. |

**Rule I held to:** no invented numbers, no "10x", no client names. The
short-stay host stays anonymous. Any stat used below is in the **Research
appendix** with a source and a date. If you can't point to the source, it
doesn't go out.

**The honest bit:** daily posting only works if most posts are genuinely
useful. If a day arrives and nothing's true to say, post nothing. A quiet
page beats a page of filler. That's why there are 30 slots but a few are
marked *optional*.

# Research appendix

**Why this exists.** Four posts above cite numbers. This is where each one comes
from, so you can defend them if someone asks "where's that from?" — and so you
never post a stat you can't source.

## Read this first — the numbers don't agree

The hardest thing about UK AI-adoption stats is that they **contradict each
other**, because they measure different populations with different definitions:

| Source | Says | Who they asked |
|---|---|---|
| DSIT (Jan 2026) | **16%** of businesses use AI | UK businesses, broad |
| YouGov (2026) | **31%** of SMEs use AI | SME decision-makers (≤250 staff) |
| BCC (Mar 2026) | **54%** of UK firms use AI | BCC survey, mostly SMEs |
| ONS (Oct 2026) | **30%** of businesses use AI | Businesses with 10+ staff |

**Why the spread:** BCC's 54% counts any firm where AI touches the work at all,
including staff using ChatGPT on their own. DSIT's 16% is a stricter definition.
Neither is wrong — they're answering different questions.

**How to handle it on LinkedIn:** never quote the highest number to sound
impressive. **Use ONS** — it's the official statistics body, it publishes its
method, and its numbers are the most defensible. If someone challenges an ONS
figure, the reply is "that's the Office for National Statistics, September 2026."
That's the end of the discussion. If you cite the 54% and they find the 16%,
you look like you picked the flattering number.

## The sources, with what each proves

**1. AI adoption is rising fast, from a low base.**
- ONS: share of businesses using at least one AI technology rose from **9%
  (Sept 2023) to 30% (Sept 2026)**. For firms with 250+ staff, 51%.
- Source: ONS, *Business insights and impact on the UK economy*, 8 October 2026.
- Use it to say: this is early, and moving quickly.

**2. Most AI use in small firms is generic, not built for them.**
- The BCC/University of Essex study: of 363 AI-using firms, **298 were using
  generic tools only**. Around **10% of all firms** had adopted anything bespoke.
- Source: Bharier, Etheridge & Morais, *AI Adoption and Workforce Restructuring
  in SMEs*, ISER working paper 2026/01, BCC Business Outlook Survey, Jan 2026
  (N=672).
- Use it to say: the gap isn't access to AI. It's someone fixing *your* specific
  process. This is the single most useful stat for the WebAwesome offer.

**3. Small firms lag large ones.**
- ONS (Oct 2026): **~29% of businesses with fewer than 10 employees** use AI,
  against 51% of those with 100+.
- Source: same ONS bulletin as row 1.
- Use it to say: the smallest businesses have the most room to get ahead.

**4. Firms use AI for operations, not moonshots.**
- ONS (June 2026): improving business operations is the most common use,
  reported by around **three-fifths** of AI-using businesses.
- Source: ONS, *Artificial intelligence in UK businesses, 2023 to 2026*,
  16 July 2026.
- Use it to say: the boring, practical use is the norm — not the exception.

**5. AI mostly hasn't cut jobs in SMEs — yet.**
- BCC (Mar 2026): **95% of SMEs using AI report no impact on workforce size**;
  86% say job roles are unchanged.
- Source: BCC, *Future of Work: AI in the Workplace*, 18 March 2026.
- Use it to say: the "AI will replace us" panic is ahead of the evidence. Useful
  for both audiences — reassuring for WA clients, honest for MBA mentees.

**6. Business analysis is a live, paying market.**
- **20,362 unique job postings** for business analysis roles, Jan–Apr 2026;
  Adzuna rated demand 'HIGH'. Mean UK salary **£56,743**, up 4.6% YoY, and
  31.1% above the national average. 49.4% of roles offer flexible/remote working.
- Sources: Adzuna data via Learning People, *Business Analytics Job Market
  Report*, June 2026. Salary cross-check: IT Jobs Watch — median **Business
  Analyst £55,000** (6 months to 30 Sept 2026).
- Use it to say: this is a field with demand, not a consolation prize for
  people who couldn't get into tech. Strongest stat for the mentoringba audience.

**7. AI user numbers are high among employees, low among firms.**
- ONS (June 2026): **over half of employees** report using AI, against **over a
  third of businesses**. US comparison: 43% of US workers used generative AI in
  early 2026.
- Source: ONS, *Artificial intelligence in UK businesses*, July 2026.
- Use it to say: people are using AI whether or not their employer has a plan.
  That gap is where consultants and trainers live.

## Stats I deliberately did NOT use

- **"Small businesses saved £273.5 billion annually"** (SBEC) — 2023, US, and it
  extrapolates a small survey to a national number. Not defensible.
- **"54% of SMEs use AI"** on its own — true to its source, but it collapses
  under comparison to ONS/DSIT. Fine as context with a caveat; not as a headline.
- **Any "X% of businesses fail at AI"** figure — I checked, the well-known ones
  trace back to unverifiable vendor blogs. If you see one, don't post it.
