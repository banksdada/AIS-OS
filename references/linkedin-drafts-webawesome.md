## Draft 1 — "The problem, not the AI"

*Post type: opinion / positioning. Best for reach. ~180 words.*

> Everyone is selling AI.
>
> Almost nobody is selling the problem gone.
>
> I build automations for small businesses. And the honest truth is nobody has ever come to me wanting AI.
>
> They come with a headache. Guests messaging at 2am. Leads falling through a spreadsheet. A report someone rebuilds by hand every Monday.
>
> AI is the tool. The headache is the job.
>
> This week we shipped a small thing for a short-stay host. Their booking messages now get read and drafted before breakfast, and a person still checks every one before it goes out. Two apartments. Dozens of messages. One quiet morning routine.
>
> Nobody asked for AI. They asked for their mornings back.
>
> Building an AI Operating System for my own businesses right now — same idea, pointed inwards. Automate the repeatable, keep the judgement human.
>
> Good times.
>
> #AI #Automation #SmallBusiness

## Draft 2 — "Human in the loop"

*Post type: credibility / values. ~170 words.*

A lot of AI talk skips the only part that matters for a small business: who is
still responsible when it goes wrong.

> I was told recently that AI will run businesses soon.
>
> Maybe. But I've just spent a week building a system where a person still reads every message before it leaves the building. On purpose.
>
> Here's why. When an automation drafts a reply to a guest, the risk isn't the AI being wrong. It's the business owner being asleep. The judgement has to stay human — the typing doesn't.
>
> So the rule I build by:
>
> The automation does the reading and the drafting. A person does the deciding.
>
> That's not a limitation. It's the product.
>
> Any AI that removes the human from the loop on customer conversations isn't saving you time. It's lending you risk.
>
> #Automation #AI #SmallBusiness

## Draft 3 — "Build in public: AIOS"

*Post type: journey / behind-the-scenes. ~200 words. Ties to your "working on an
AI Operating System" snippet.*

> Working on an AI Operating System and excited at what is possible.
>
> The short version: I'm wiring my own two businesses up to AI, properly. Not chat windows and prompts. A system. It checks my connections every morning, drafts the messages, keeps the client list, and tells me what actually needs me today.
>
> What I've learned so far, and I'll keep saying it:
>
> Most of the value isn't the clever bit. It's the plumbing. Getting the data in the right place, signed, trusted, so a machine can act on it safely.
>
> A signature check sounds boring. It's the difference between a lead landing in your pipeline and a stranger writing rubbish into your CRM.
>
> Day by day. One automation at a time.
>
> If you're a small business owner wondering where to start — start with the task you do three times a week. Not the exciting one. The boring one.
>
> #AIOS #Automation #SmallBusiness #BuildInPublic

## Notes for you

- **Three angles, one each week** works better than dumping all three — LinkedIn
  rewards a single clear idea per post.
- **Draft 1 is the strongest cold.** It states a position, gives one real
  example, and asks the reader nothing until the end. Start there.
- **Nothing here claims results you can't back.** The short-stay host is real
  (two apartments, daily drafting). No revenue figures, no "10x", no invented
  numbers.
- **The un-flattering lines are deliberate** — "nobody has ever come to me
  wanting AI" and "a signature check sounds boring" are what make the rest
  believable. Don't sand them off.
- **To confirm before posting:** whether you want to name the short-stay client
  or keep it anonymous (the case study decision was anonymous — these drafts
  stay anonymous to match).
